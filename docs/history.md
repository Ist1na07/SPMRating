# SPM Rating v1.0.0 History, Findings, and Methods

This document consists of three parts: the mechanical differences between this algorithm and its predecessors, the conclusions obtained during development, and the methods that keep those conclusions reliable. The last section lists future optimization directions by priority. All numbers comparing against external algorithms are Spearman rank correlations; cross-key-count comparisons use rank correlation only, because the two label sets are not on the same scale. Companion documents: the [mechanism document](mechanism.md) describes the algorithm itself; the [maintenance guide](maintenance.md) covers day-to-day use and upgrades.

## 1. Comparison Targets and Baselines

Development used three actual comparison targets.

The first is the original [sunny-rework](https://github.com/sunnyxxy/Star-Rating-Rebirth) — the published, untuned version: a single total-score output with no jack/LN split. The second is the tuned sunny-rework: identical in structure to the original, but recalibrated to a rank-tier scale on a 339-chart play dataset — five internal mechanism adjustments (removed the jack-weakening term, jack aggregation power changed from 5 to 4, stream gain coefficient doubled, column-imbalance cubic coefficient changed from 5 to 10, release-exponent steepness and bias adjusted), with all 26 tail constants refit. The third is the old SPM, i.e. the spm-rating 0.4.0 shipped with the benchmark pack: a sunny-rework base plus a batch of enhancements, the most prominent being a correction layer over whole-chart statistics. The benchmark pack contains no SPM code older than 0.4.0; "old SPM" in this document always means that version.

Baseline numbers must always be cited with the version, because the gap between the original and the tuned version exceeds this algorithm's own improvement. On 7-key the strongest is the tuned version at 0.97921, while the original reaches only 0.97129; on 4-key the strongest is the original at 0.97911, while the tuned version reaches only 0.97134, because the tuned version was calibrated on 7-key only. The old SPM scores 0.98373 on 7-key, but with a built-in advantage: its 7-key scores and the benchmark labels come out of the same calibration pipeline. SPM Rating v1.0.0 scores 0.98449 on 7-key and 0.98535 on 4-key, exceeding the strongest baselines by 0.0053 and 0.0062 respectively. On a 6-key dataset that never took part in tuning, v1.0.0's zero-shot score is 0.95796, above the sunny-rework original's reference of 0.95515.

## 2. What Changed Mechanically Relative to sunny

sunny's pipeline: parse the chart, lay out a corner grid, compute six channels — jack, cross-column transition, density, column imbalance, rhythmic unevenness, LN release — merge them into a total difficulty curve, aggregate by percentile, then add short-chart normalization, top-end compression, and global scaling. SPM Rating retains this skeleton, but nearly every channel has been modified, and four groups of mechanisms with no sunny counterpart were added.

The first new group is the three-way jack split. sunny has a single jack channel; SPM Rating splits it into isolated mini-jacks, chorded heavy jacks, and single-column long runs, each with its own modulation shape, plus a same-column triplet gain, a same-column fast-pair supplement, and a same-hand chord discount. The long-run channel was later proven redundant — its weight is effectively zero and its curve is kept only for the locked-anchor term; see Section 4 for the detailed finding.

The second new group is the second-generation LN coordination channel. sunny has no LN coordination channel. SPM Rating's first-generation implementation used the total hold count as a scalar modulation, which cannot tell which column is being held — yet which finger is held is the entire meaning of locked-hand difficulty. The second generation uses a cross-column cost matrix instead, where the cost depends on the identity of the held column; straddle-jack patterns became expressible for the first time.

The third new group is the hybrid-LN supplements, targeting the gap on 7-key hybrid and LN charts: four curves for release crowding, hold duration, double-press density, and recovery discount.

The fourth new group is the visual regularity channel, which measures how well the chart's rhythm aligns with the local beat and enters the total difficulty as a density-gated interaction. Its positive coefficient is a major finding: regular charts are systematically underestimated by the hand model and need extra credit rather than a reading discount.

Rewritten or strengthened channels include LN release, cross-column transition, density, rhythmic unevenness, and aggregation. LN release grew from a single tail-to-tail pricing into three parts — per-tail release, tail-to-tail sequences, and release-order triples — with coordination weights distinguishing same-column, same-hand, thumb, and cross-hand cases. Cross-column transition went from fixed boundary weights to tunable parameters, adding a geometric jump channel and an inward/outward direction split — transitions landing toward or away from the thumb are priced separately. The density channel gained an LN-body bonus, chord coupling, and short-burst terms. Aggregation moved from percentiles to a weighted power mean with hold downweighting and a peak envelope, plus chart-level calibration; 4-key charts use their own set of per-channel coefficients, every channel has key-count override parameters, and each channel has its own hit-tolerance response coefficient. On the engineering side, flattened whole-library computation replaced per-chart loops — the full 7-key library completes in seconds — which is the prerequisite for large-scale joint tuning. The five smoothing switches for timestamp discreteness are a robustness layer unique to SPM Rating, and one of the largest behavioral differences from the entire sunny family.

## 3. What Was Inherited, Improved, and Removed Relative to the Old SPM

The old SPM was itself an enhanced sunny: hand-distance weights on the cross-column channel, a fully rewritten LN release, a shield term around LN heads, an inversion-modifier term (same-column spike minus a Gaussian-guided dip), and aggregation changed to a sigmoid accuracy model; the largest single item was a 9-feature chart-level scalar correction layer — several whole-chart statistics, linearly weighted to adjust the star rating directly.

SPM Rating inherits the pipeline skeleton and the power-mean orders of magnitude; aggregation moved from the percentile and sigmoid schemes to a weighted power mean with hold downweighting and a peak envelope. The improvements: the shield grew from an exponential term around LN heads into a second-generation shield (same-column predecessor plus hold amplification); LN release grew from an object-oriented rewrite into three parts plus continuity; cross-column went from hand-distance weights to tunable boundary groups, a jump channel, and inward/outward splits; the inversion-modifier idea was absorbed into the fast-jack term and the recovery discount. What was deleted is the entire chart-level correction layer. This algorithm's design principle is that difficulty may only arise from the chart's local time structure; a correction layer that adds whole-chart statistics directly into the star rating conflicts with that principle, so the whole layer was removed and every such phenomenon was rebuilt as a time curve. The stamina term, already disabled when the old SPM shipped, is likewise not adopted.

The remaining gap must also be stated plainly. For reference, an implementation using a whole-chart correction layer reaches 0.98674 on 7-key and 0.98639 on 4-key on the same data; this release trails it by 0.0023 and 0.0010, and that difference is mostly the correction layer. Closing to this distance under a time-series-features-only constraint, while pulling ahead on 6-key data that never took part in training, is this project's final form. Going further requires new physical quantities — it cannot be done by tuning alone.

## 4. Development Process and Key Findings

The tuning protocol was one and the same throughout. The objective is a pairwise ranking loss, plus auxiliary ranking losses computed per chart category, plus a 7-key dead-zone loss (no loss when the label gap is below a threshold), with 7-key and 4-key merged by weight. The optimizer is block coordinate descent with a Nelder-Mead simplex search per block; parameters affecting data structures stay frozen in the outer layer and are adjusted by grid plus inner search. Computation uses a sharded process pool and a curve-fingerprint cache; a merge-layer evaluation takes under a second. The shared backbone is only ever adjusted on the joint two-key-count objective, never on single-key-count data alone.

Development ran in four phases. The first phase established the key-count split, visual regularity, the peak envelope, hybrid LN, and the three-way jack split, scoring about 0.005 above the strongest baseline; the same phase falsified LN-tail clipping — all three doses monotonically harmful, so paper length is the correct choice. The second phase fixed the wiring of four dead parameters and reconciled all blocks, with all four cross-validation folds improving. The third phase tuned the cross-column block that several rounds of joint tuning had missed — the largest single-round gain, about +0.002 on 7-key — then added the inward/outward split and broke the tie with the control on 6-key. The fourth phase enabled all smoothing switches and retuned with an equal budget, producing this release. Compared with the end of the third phase, 7-key is 0.0021 lower and 4-key 0.0004 lower, while the unseen 6-key set a new high and the perturbation checks improved substantially; this tradeoff was written down before the experiments began — see the second methodology item.

Mechanisms falsified along the way are assets as well: LN-tail clipping, lock-power saturation, the visual channel's max-versus-power-mean combination, seven low-correlation visual features, lock-condition gating, fatigue memory kernels, the holds-exceed-taps row gate, hybrid 4-key overrides, the long-run merge channel, cross-column pair-override expansion — over ten items, each with control experiments on file. The deepest lesson came from the fatigue kernels: all 23 kernel shapes were screened, none usable on 7-key; 4-key's only signal beat the random null yet lost to the smoothness-preserving cyclic-shift null — a chart-level distribution channel disguised as a time-series channel, banned by the design principle.

Several attribution findings changed engineering practice. The hold-downweight coefficient fixed at its lower bound is a true optimum: free walks leave the bounds only slightly, and widening them gains nothing. The hold-and-tap negative coefficient is an interior optimum whose hard-clamp selectivity is irreplaceable — every softer reparameterization performed worse. The twist ratio's negative jack coefficient is a gate between jack sections and technical sections; removing it worsens the objective, and the tuner keeps pushing it more negative.

## 5. Methodology

First, the validation ladder. A candidate mechanism must first pass null-hypothesis calibration: it has to beat the random null, and the fatigue-kernel episode proves that is not enough — the smoothness-preserving cyclic-shift null is the decisive one: randomly shifting the curve and then measuring the gain rules out pseudo-channels that only reflect the overall distribution. Then a low-cost block refit checks whether the mechanism is redundant once neighboring parameters can move. Finally a same-budget joint ablation, with control and experiment deep-tuned on equal compute. A result tuned on only one side is not evidence.

Second, pre-registered decision criteria. Adoption conditions are written before the experiment: meet the bar and it is adopted, miss it and it is rejected, ties are decided by 6-key. Rejected versions are recorded too — they evidence the objective function's multimodality.

Third, 6-key as arbiter. 6-key labels are coarse and the dataset was pre-screened by sunny, so training on it risks circularity — it serves as a zero-shot arbiter only, never as a training signal. It broke one tie and witnessed generalization at every phase.

Fourth, cross-validation and sensitivity. Four-fold full refits; the validation-versus-full gap is about 0.001 on 7-key and about 0.0006 on 4-key, the smallest of any generation. Perturbing load-bearing parameters by ±5% moves scores by at most 0.00066 — the optimum sits in a flat basin.

Fifth, perturbation checks. Adding ±1 ms perturbations to all timestamps is the effective detector for mechanism overfitting; neither cross-validation nor 6-key trends can detect it. The target is a post-perturbation median error under 0.05. Measured at release: 0.178 on 7-key, 0.039 on 4-key — 4-key passes, 7-key does not, recorded as is.

Sixth, retirement discipline. Falsified mechanisms are removed and archived; dead parameters are named in the documentation so that nobody retunes ghost parameters.

## 6. Possible Future Improvements

Ordered by expected value.

First is a mechanism redesign of the LN release channel — the only direction requiring new physics. At release the 7-key perturbation median is still above the ideal; tightening the release-gap floor gets tuned right back, showing that the release pricing's functional form itself needs replacing, not its parameters. Candidates include a perceptual scale function for release gaps, unified tail-to-note and tail-to-tail handling, and the contribution form of isolated tails.

Second is mid-band ordering on 7-key hybrid and LN charts. Charts clearly good or bad at both ends already rank correctly; the middle band is near the current architecture's ceiling and needs new mid-band mechanism research, not tuning.

Third is within-group ordering at the 4-key speed top end. The peak envelope fixed part of it; the remainder needs discriminating mechanisms within the top end.

Fourth is multi-start micro-perturbation restarts. The objective's multimodality is evidenced in two directions; restarts are low-cost and low-risk, and every restart must pass both the cross-validation and 6-key checks.

Fifth is shape refinement of hybrid and LN mechanisms, including the saturation forms of release crowding and hold duration, and making the hold-and-tap gate explicit. The expected gains here are near zero — hygiene refactors.

Directions not recommended are equally explicit: 6-key as a training signal risks circularity; the density and release hard clamps are load-bearing, and changing them degrades performance clearly; the hold-downweight bounds are verified and widening them gains nothing; the residual errors of a few top-end LN charts appear on only three charts, and any fix would require whole-chart-level selective correction, conflicting with this algorithm's design principle.
